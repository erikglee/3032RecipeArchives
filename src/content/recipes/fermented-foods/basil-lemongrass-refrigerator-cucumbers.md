ingredients:
  - Small cucumbers, enough to fill several jars
  - 6 cups 5% acidity vinegar, white or rice vinegar
  - 6 cups water
  - 125 g kosher or pickling salt
  - 3 tablespoons sugar
  - 5 to 6 lemongrass stalks, lower portions crushed and cut into 3-inch pieces
  - 12 to 16 garlic cloves, lightly smashed
  - 3 to 4 inches fresh ginger, thinly sliced
  - 1 1/2 to 2 tablespoons coriander seeds
  - 1 tablespoon pink peppercorns, or black peppercorns
  - 1 1/2 teaspoons ground turmeric, or 2 inches fresh turmeric, thinly sliced
  - 3 to 6 hot peppers, lightly slit
  - 2 to 3 generous handfuls fresh basil
  - 15 to 20 fresh curry leaves, optional
  - 6 to 10 makrut lime leaves, optional

## Make and cool the brine

1. Start a day ahead if possible, so the brine has time to cool completely before it reaches the cucumbers.
2. Combine the vinegar, water, salt, and sugar. Warm only a portion of the liquid, just enough to dissolve the salt and sugar, then combine it with the remaining cool liquid.
3. Let the brine cool to at least room temperature, ideally 70 to 80°F, or chill it in the refrigerator for the best chance at a crisp pickle.

## Pack the cucumbers

1. Wash the cucumbers well and trim about 1/8 inch from both ends. Leave them whole.
2. Divide the lemongrass, garlic, ginger, coriander, peppercorns, turmeric, hot peppers, basil, and optional curry or lime leaves among clean jars. Pack the cucumbers in tightly.
3. Pour in enough completely cooled brine to submerge the cucumbers. Refrigerate immediately.

## Pickle and store

1. The cucumbers will be lightly pickled after 2 to 3 days, good around day 5, and at their best around day 7.
2. Keep refrigerated and use clean utensils. For the best texture and flavor, eat within 3 to 4 weeks.

## Notes

Make the aromatics do the work in the jars; any unused plain brine can be refrigerated for another batch. Cold brine helps preserve crunch and keeps the basil and curry leaves fresher.
