ingredients:
  - 1 1/2 pounds pork ribs or bone-in pork shoulder
  - 8 cups water or light pork or chicken stock
  - 1 smoked kielbasa, sliced
  - 1 1/2 to 2 cups sauerkraut, roughly chopped, with liquid reserved
  - 1 medium onion, diced
  - 1 carrot, sliced
  - 1 celery stalk, sliced
  - 2 medium potatoes, diced
  - 2 garlic cloves, minced
  - 2 bay leaves
  - 3 to 4 whole allspice berries
  - 1/2 teaspoon black pepper
  - 1/2 teaspoon caraway seeds, optional
  - Salt, to taste
  - 1 tablespoon butter or pork fat
  - Fresh dill or parsley, optional

## Build the broth

1. Put the pork in a large pot and cover with the water or stock. Bring to a gentle boil, skimming any foam from the surface.
2. Reduce to a low simmer, partially cover, and cook for 45 to 60 minutes, until the pork is tender.
3. Remove the pork, let it cool slightly, then cut it into bite-size pieces and return it to the pot.

## Add sauerkraut and spices

1. Add the sauerkraut, bay leaves, allspice, black pepper, and caraway, if using.
2. Simmer for 30 minutes, allowing the sauerkraut to soften and season the broth. If the sauerkraut is especially sharp, rinse half of it before adding it to the soup.

## Cook the vegetables

1. Melt the butter or pork fat in a small pan. Saute the onion, carrot, and celery over medium heat for 8 to 10 minutes, until soft and lightly golden.
2. Add the garlic and cook for 30 seconds, then transfer the vegetables to the soup.
3. Add the potatoes and kielbasa. Simmer for 20 to 25 minutes, until the potatoes are tender.

## Finish and serve

1. Taste before adding salt, since the sauerkraut and kielbasa are already salty. Add reserved sauerkraut liquid a tablespoon at a time if you want more tang.
2. Remove the bay leaves and allspice. Garnish with dill or parsley, if using.
3. Serve with rye bread or dark sourdough, black pepper at the table, and sour cream on the side if you like.

## Notes

The finished soup should be savory and smoky first, with a rounded sauerkraut tang rather than harsh acidity. The ribs make the broth rich and cohesive, while the kielbasa provides distinct smoky bites. Like many soups of this kind, it tastes even better after a day in the refrigerator and freezes well.
