  - 3 pounds oxtail pieces
  - 1 1/2 teaspoons salt
  - 1 teaspoon black pepper
  - 1 teaspoon allspice
  - 1 teaspoon smoked or sweet paprika
  - 1 tablespoon brown sugar
  - 2 tablespoons soy sauce, divided
  - 2 teaspoons Worcestershire sauce, divided
  - 4 garlic cloves, minced
  - 1 tablespoon grated ginger
  - 4 to 5 scallions, chopped
  - 1 small onion, chopped
  - A few sprigs fresh thyme
  - 1/2 to 1 Scotch bonnet or habanero, optional
  - 1 to 2 tablespoons neutral oil
  - 2 cups beef stock
  - 1 to 2 tablespoons ketchup or tomato paste
  - 1 to 2 carrots, cut into chunks
  - 1 can butter beans, drained and rinsed
  - Lime, optional, for finishing

## Season the oxtail

1. Combine the oxtail with the salt, pepper, allspice, paprika, brown sugar, 1 tablespoon soy sauce, 1 teaspoon Worcestershire, garlic, ginger, scallions, onion, thyme, and Scotch bonnet or habanero, if using.
2. Marinate for a few hours or overnight when possible. The oxtail can also be cooked right away.

## Brown and build the braise

1. Heat the oil in a heavy Dutch oven over medium-high heat. Lift the oxtail from the marinade, scraping off excess aromatics so they do not burn.
2. Brown the oxtail deeply on all sides, working in batches if needed. Transfer it to a plate.
3. Lower the heat to medium. Add the reserved onion, scallion, garlic, ginger, and thyme to the pot and saute for a few minutes.
4. Stir in the stock, ketchup or tomato paste, remaining soy sauce and Worcestershire, carrots, and optional whole Scotch bonnet. Scrape up any browned bits from the bottom of the pot.

## Braise until tender

1. Return the oxtail and any accumulated juices to the pot. The liquid should reach roughly one-third to one-half of the way up the meat; add a little water or stock if needed.
2. Cover and braise in a 280°F oven for 5 hours, turning the pieces once or twice if practical. Check the liquid level partway through and add a splash of stock or water if it is getting low.
3. The oxtail is ready when a fork slides in easily and the meat is loosening from the bones.

## Finish with beans

1. During the last 20 to 30 minutes, stir in the butter beans.
2. Leave the lid partly off near the end, if needed, so the braising liquid reduces into a thick, glossy gravy.
3. Taste and adjust with salt, pepper, soy sauce, or Worcestershire. Add a small squeeze of lime if the gravy needs brightness.

## Notes

Serve over rice to catch the gravy. Oxtail is naturally rich; skim excess fat while cooking, or refrigerate the finished dish overnight and lift off the hardened fat before reheating.
