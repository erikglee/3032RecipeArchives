          Rosemary Toffee Shortbread

          Orange-scented shortbread with homemade toffee and rosemary, pressed into cheerful holiday shapes.

          Prep: 45 minutes, plus cooling and chilling · Cook: 25 minutes · Serves: 12 cookies

          Ingredients

          • Shortbread: 1 stick softened butter
          • Shortbread: 55g sugar
          • Shortbread: 145g flour
          • Shortbread: zest of 1 orange
          • Shortbread: 45g toffee, crushed
          • Shortbread: 1 sprig rosemary, finely chopped (about 2g)
          • Toffee: 165g brown sugar
          • Toffee: 6 tablespoons butter
          • Toffee: 2 tablespoons water

          Make the toffee 1. Melt the brown sugar, butter, and water in a saucepan over medium heat, stirring until dissolved. 2. Bring to a boil. Cook, swirling the saucepan occasionally, until the bubbles slow and the mixture reaches 290 F, about 6 to 8 minutes. 3. Pour onto a parchment-lined baking sheet. Let cool completely, then break or cut into small pieces. ## Make the cookies 1. Rub the orange zest and finely chopped rosemary into the sugar until it feels like wet sand. 2. Cream the sugar mixture and softened butter until fluffy. 3. Add the flour and mix only until just combined. 4. Fold in the crushed toffee. 5. Roll the dough between two sheets of parchment to about 1/4 inch thick. 6. Chill for 30 minutes, then cut into shapes with a favorite cookie cutter or a 2-inch round cutter. 7. Bake at 365 F for 16 minutes, until the bottoms are golden. ## Notes Cool completely before eating so the toffee can set. The homemade toffee is adapted from this [NYT Cooking toffee recipe](https://cooking.nytimes.com/recipes/761573017-toffee); store-bought toffee works nicely when you want to skip the homemade batch.

          Butter Chicken with Garden Tomatoes

          Charred yogurt-marinated chicken in a plush tomato, butter, and cream sauce that uses garden tomatoes alongside canned tomatoes for fresh depth.

          Developed with ChatGPT.

          Prep: 30 minutes, plus marinating · Cook: 50 minutes · Serves: 4 to 6

          Ingredients

          • 1 1/2 to 2 pounds boneless, skinless chicken thighs, cut into large bite-size pieces
          • 1/2 cup full-fat plain Greek yogurt
          • 1 tablespoon lemon juice
          • 7 garlic cloves, divided, grated or minced
          • 2 1/2 tablespoons fresh ginger, divided, grated
          • 3 teaspoons garam masala, divided
          • 2 1/2 teaspoons ground cumin, divided
          • 2 teaspoons ground coriander, divided
          • 1 to 1 1/2 teaspoons Kashmiri chili powder or mild paprika, divided
          • 1 teaspoon ground turmeric, divided
          • 2 1/4 teaspoons kosher salt, plus more to taste
          • 1 tablespoon neutral oil, plus more for cooking
          • 2 medium garden tomatoes, roughly chopped
          • 1 can diced tomatoes, 14 to 15 ounces
          • 2 tablespoons tomato paste
          • 4 tablespoons butter, divided, plus 1 tablespoon cold butter optional
          • 1 medium onion, finely chopped
          • 1 to 2 teaspoons sugar or honey
          • 3/4 to 1 cup heavy cream
          • 1 teaspoon dried fenugreek leaves, crushed
          • 1/2 teaspoon ground cardamom or seeds from 3 green cardamom pods, optional
          • Water, as needed

          Marinate the chicken 1. Combine the chicken, yogurt, lemon juice, 3 garlic cloves, 1 tablespoon ginger, 1 1/2 teaspoons garam masala, 1 teaspoon cumin, 1 teaspoon coriander, 1 teaspoon Kashmiri chili powder or paprika, 1/2 teaspoon turmeric, 1 1/4 teaspoons salt, and 1 tablespoon oil. 2. Refrigerate for at least 30 minutes, or 2 to 8 hours for more flavor. ## Char the chicken 1. Heat a large skillet, cast-iron pan, grill, or griddle until quite hot. Add a little oil and cook the chicken in batches for 2 to 3 minutes per side, until it has dark browned or charred spots but is not necessarily cooked through. 2. Transfer the chicken to a plate. Keep the browned bits in the pan. ## Make the tomato sauce 1. Lower the heat to medium. Add 2 tablespoons butter and the onion. Cook for 7 to 10 minutes, until very soft and beginning to turn golden. 2. Add the remaining garlic and ginger; cook for 1 minute. Add the tomato paste and cook, stirring frequently, for 2 to 3 minutes until it darkens to a rusty red. 3. Add the remaining garam masala, cumin, coriander, chili powder or paprika, turmeric, and optional cardamom. Cook for 30 seconds. 4. Add the garden tomatoes, canned tomatoes, and 1/2 teaspoon salt. Cook uncovered for 15 to 20 minutes, until the fresh tomatoes break down and the sauce is concentrated. 5. Blend the sauce until completely smooth with an immersion or countertop blender. Return it to the pan. ## Finish the butter chicken 1. Stir in the cream, remaining 2 tablespoons butter, fenugreek leaves, and 1 teaspoon sugar or honey. Taste and add more sugar or honey if the tomatoes are especially tart. 2. Add the chicken and any accumulated juices. Simmer gently for 5 to 8 minutes, until the chicken is cooked through. Add water a few tablespoons at a time if the sauce is too thick, or simmer uncovered if it is too thin. 3. For an especially glossy finish, turn off the heat and stir in 1 tablespoon cold butter. Serve with basmati rice or naan and a small squeeze of lemon at the table. ## Notes The defining details are true browning on the chicken, well-cooked tomato paste, and kasuri methi. The sauce keeps well and often tastes even better the next day.

          Swedish Meatballs

          Tender beef-and-pork meatballs in a savory cream gravy, from a well-used family recipe card.

          Prep: 35 minutes · Cook: 45 minutes, plus optional 30-minute bake · Serves: 12 servings

          Ingredients

          • 1 cup fine bread crumbs
          • 2 1/2 cups milk, divided
          • 1 1/2 pounds ground beef
          • 1/2 pound ground pork
          • 1 cup finely chopped onion
          • 2 eggs, lightly beaten
          • 1 1/2 teaspoons salt
          • 1/4 teaspoon black pepper
          • 3 to 4 teaspoons freshly ground nutmeg
          • 1/2 cup butter or margarine
          • 1/4 cup flour
          • 2 to 3 beef bouillon cubes
          • 1 1/2 cups hot water
          • 1 1/2 cups light cream

          Make the meatballs 1. Soften the bread crumbs in 1 cup of the milk. 2. Add the ground beef, ground pork, onion, eggs, salt, pepper, and nutmeg. Mix thoroughly, then shape into 1-inch meatballs. An ice-cream scoop is useful for portioning. 3. Heat the butter in a large skillet over medium heat. Brown the meatballs in batches, turning them until browned on all sides. Transfer the browned meatballs to a plate. ## Make the cream gravy 1. Stir the flour into the skillet drippings and cook, stirring, until blended. 2. Dissolve the bouillon cubes in the hot water. Gradually add the bouillon to the flour mixture, stirring constantly until smooth. 3. Stir in the remaining 1 1/2 cups milk and the light cream. Cook over low heat, stirring constantly, for about 3 minutes or until smooth. ## Finish and serve 1. Return the meatballs to the gravy. Simmer gently for 10 to 15 minutes, stirring occasionally, until cooked through. 2. For the family card's baked finish, transfer the meatballs and gravy to an oven-safe dish and bake at 350F for 30 minutes before serving. ## Family notes - The typed card's "f;pir" is flour. - Use a 3:1 ratio of ground beef to ground pork: 1 1/2 pounds beef and 1/2 pound pork for this batch. - The card calls for 1 teaspoon nutmeg (or allspice), but this family version uses 3 to 4 teaspoons of nutmeg. Fresh nutmeg makes a notable difference; replace an older jar when its aroma has faded.

          Crispy Chicken with White-Wine Mushroom, Chickpea & Spinach Broth

          Crisp-skinned chicken thighs over a savory, lemony broth of mushrooms, chickpeas, spinach, and white wine.

          Developed with ChatGPT.

          Prep: 20 minutes · Cook: 1 hour · Serves: 3 to 4

          Ingredients

          • 4 bone-in, skin-on chicken thighs
          • 2 cans chickpeas, drained and rinsed
          • 8 to 12 ounces mushrooms, sliced
          • 1 medium yellow onion, diced
          • 4 to 5 garlic cloves, minced
          • 1/2 cup dry white wine
          • 3 cups chicken broth
          • 2 to 3 large handfuls spinach
          • 1 to 2 teaspoons fresh thyme leaves
          • 1 tablespoon butter
          • 1 lemon
          • Olive oil, if needed
          • Kosher salt and lots of black pepper
          • Red pepper flakes, optional
          • Parmesan, optional

          Crisp the chicken 1. Pat the chicken thighs very dry and season generously with salt and pepper. 2. Set the thighs skin-side down in a cold skillet. Turn the heat to medium and cook without moving them for 25 minutes, until the skin is deeply golden and crisp. 3. Flip and cook for 5 to 7 minutes more, then transfer the chicken to a plate. Leave the rendered fat in the skillet. ## Brown the mushrooms 1. Turn the heat to medium-high. Add the mushrooms in an even layer and leave them alone for 2 to 3 minutes before stirring. 2. Continue cooking for 3 to 4 minutes, until deeply browned. Add the onion with a pinch of salt and cook until softened, about 4 to 5 minutes. 3. Stir in the garlic, thyme, black pepper, and red pepper flakes, if using. Cook for 30 to 60 seconds. ## Build the broth 1. Pour in the white wine and scrape up the browned bits. Simmer until reduced by about half, 2 to 3 minutes. 2. Add the chickpeas and chicken broth. Bring to a simmer, then smash about one-quarter to one-third of the chickpeas against the side of the pan with a spoon or potato masher. 3. Simmer uncovered for 12 to 15 minutes, until the broth is lightly thickened. ## Finish and serve 1. Stir in the spinach until just wilted, 1 to 2 minutes. Turn off the heat and stir in the butter, juice of half the lemon, a little zest, and plenty of black pepper. Taste and adjust the salt and lemon. 2. Nestle the chicken into the skillet skin-side up, keeping the broth below the skin. If needed, finish in a 400 F oven for 5 to 10 minutes, until the chicken reaches 165 F at its thickest point. 3. Serve the broth in shallow bowls with a chicken thigh on top. Finish with more lemon, thyme, Parmesan, olive oil, and crusty bread. ## Notes For an extra savory finish, stir 1 to 2 teaspoons of Dijon mustard into the broth just before serving.

            Jamaican-Style Oxtail with Butter Beans

            Deeply browned oxtail braised until tender in a savory, gently sweet gravy, with butter beans added near the end.

            Developed with ChatGPT.

            Prep: 40 minutes, plus optional marinating · Cook: 5 hours 45 minutes · Serves: 4 to 6

            Ingredients

            • 3 pounds oxtail pieces
            • 1 1/2 teaspoons salt
            • 1 teaspoon black pepper
            • 1 teaspoon allspice
            • 1 teaspoon smoked or sweet paprika
            • 1 tablespoon brown sugar
            • 2 tablespoons soy sauce, divided
            • 2 teaspoons Worcestershire sauce, divided
            • 4 garlic cloves, minced
            • 1 tablespoon grated ginger
            • 4 to 5 scallions, chopped
            • 1 small onion, chopped
            • A few sprigs fresh thyme
            • 1/2 to 1 Scotch bonnet or habanero, optional
            • 1 to 2 tablespoons neutral oil
            • 2 cups beef stock
            • 1 to 2 tablespoons ketchup or tomato paste
            • 1 to 2 carrots, cut into chunks
            • 1 can butter beans, drained and rinsed
            • Lime, optional, for finishing

            Season the oxtail 1. Combine the oxtail with the salt, pepper, allspice, paprika, brown sugar, 1 tablespoon soy sauce, 1 teaspoon Worcestershire, garlic, ginger, scallions, onion, thyme, and Scotch bonnet or habanero, if using. 2. Marinate for a few hours or overnight when possible. The oxtail can also be cooked right away. ## Brown and build the braise 1. Heat the oil in a heavy Dutch oven over medium-high heat. Lift the oxtail from the marinade, scraping off excess aromatics so they do not burn. 2. Brown the oxtail deeply on all sides, working in batches if needed. Transfer it to a plate. 3. Lower the heat to medium. Add the reserved onion, scallion, garlic, ginger, and thyme to the pot and saute for a few minutes. 4. Stir in the stock, ketchup or tomato paste, remaining soy sauce and Worcestershire, carrots, and optional whole Scotch bonnet. Scrape up any browned bits from the bottom of the pot. ## Braise until tender 1. Return the oxtail and any accumulated juices to the pot. The liquid should reach roughly one-third to one-half of the way up the meat; add a little water or stock if needed. 2. Cover and braise in a 280°F oven for 5 hours, turning the pieces once or twice if practical. Check the liquid level partway through and add a splash of stock or water if it is getting low. 3. The oxtail is ready when a fork slides in easily and the meat is loosening from the bones. ## Finish with beans 1. During the last 20 to 30 minutes, stir in the butter beans. 2. Leave the lid partly off near the end, if needed, so the braising liquid reduces into a thick, glossy gravy. 3. Taste and adjust with salt, pepper, soy sauce, or Worcestershire. Add a small squeeze of lime if the gravy needs brightness. ## Notes Serve over rice to catch the gravy. Oxtail is naturally rich; skim excess fat while cooking, or refrigerate the finished dish overnight and lift off the hardened fat before reheating.

                Basil-Lemongrass Refrigerator Cucumbers

                Crisp whole cucumbers with lemongrass, basil, ginger, turmeric, and chiles in a bright, aromatic refrigerator-pickle brine.

                Developed with ChatGPT.

                Prep: 45 minutes, plus cooling and pickling time · Cook: No cooking required · Serves: Several jars

                Ingredients

                • Small cucumbers, enough to fill several jars
                • 6 cups 5% acidity vinegar, white or rice vinegar
                • 6 cups water
                • 125 g kosher or pickling salt
                • 3 tablespoons sugar
                • 5 to 6 lemongrass stalks, lower portions crushed and cut into 3-inch pieces
                • 12 to 16 garlic cloves, lightly smashed
                • 3 to 4 inches fresh ginger, thinly sliced
                • 1 1/2 to 2 tablespoons coriander seeds
                • 1 tablespoon pink peppercorns, or black peppercorns
                • 1 1/2 teaspoons ground turmeric, or 2 inches fresh turmeric, thinly sliced
                • 3 to 6 hot peppers, lightly slit
                • 2 to 3 generous handfuls fresh basil
                • 15 to 20 fresh curry leaves, optional
                • 6 to 10 makrut lime leaves, optional

                Make and cool the brine 1. Start a day ahead if possible, so the brine has time to cool completely before it reaches the cucumbers. 2. Combine the vinegar, water, salt, and sugar. Warm only a portion of the liquid, just enough to dissolve the salt and sugar, then combine it with the remaining cool liquid. 3. Let the brine cool to at least room temperature, ideally 70 to 80°F, or chill it in the refrigerator for the best chance at a crisp pickle. ## Pack the cucumbers 1. Wash the cucumbers well and trim about 1/8 inch from both ends. Leave them whole. 2. Divide the lemongrass, garlic, ginger, coriander, peppercorns, turmeric, hot peppers, basil, and optional curry or lime leaves among clean jars. Pack the cucumbers in tightly. 3. Pour in enough completely cooled brine to submerge the cucumbers. Refrigerate immediately. ## Pickle and store 1. The cucumbers will be lightly pickled after 2 to 3 days, good around day 5, and at their best around day 7. 2. Keep refrigerated and use clean utensils. For the best texture and flavor, eat within 3 to 4 weeks. ## Notes Make the aromatics do the work in the jars; any unused plain brine can be refrigerated for another batch. Cold brine helps preserve crunch and keeps the basil and curry leaves fresher.

                Kimchi 2026

                A 2.5-gallon napa cabbage kimchi batch with daikon, carrots, and a ginger-garlic pepper paste.

                Prep: 4 hours · Cook: Brief stovetop slurry · Serves: 1 2.5-gallon crock

                Ingredients

                • 5.5kg napa cabbage, after removing outer leaves (6 very small cabbages and 1 larger cabbage)
                • 120g salt
                • 160g ginger (the batch used ginger that was not the best quality)
                • 400g onion
                • 280g garlic
                • 250g green onion
                • 3 1/2 cups water
                • 60g sugar
                • 50g glutinous rice flour
                • 300g Korean pepper flakes
                • 125g fish sauce
                • 5 daikon radishes (weight unknown)
                • Carrots (weight unknown)

                Instructions 1. Remove the outer leaves from the napa cabbage. Quarter each cabbage while keeping the core intact, then rinse the quarters. 2. Salt the individual cabbage leaves and massage them. Continue massaging every 30 minutes for 2 hours. 3. Make the slurry: boil the water, sugar, and glutinous rice flour together, whisking until incorporated. Let the mixture cool to room temperature before continuing. 4. Blitz the ginger, onion, and garlic in a food processor. 5. Once the cabbages have been salted, cut the cores from each quarter. Cut each cored quarter into three lengthwise sections. 6. Once the slurry is at room temperature, mix it with the Korean pepper flakes and the ginger-onion-garlic mixture. 7. Combine all ingredients, then transfer the kimchi to a crock. ## Fermentation notes Ferment in the crock for 9 days.                     Kapusniak: Sauerkraut Soup with Pork Ribs & Kielbasa

                    A rich, gently tangy Polish-style soup with tender pork, smoky kielbasa, soft potatoes, and sauerkraut in a deep, comforting broth.

                    Developed with ChatGPT.

                    Prep: 30 minutes · Cook: 2 hours 15 minutes · Serves: 4 to 6

                    Ingredients

                    • 1 1/2 pounds pork ribs or bone-in pork shoulder
                    • 8 cups water or light pork or chicken stock
                    • 1 smoked kielbasa, sliced
                    • 1 1/2 to 2 cups sauerkraut, roughly chopped, with liquid reserved
                    • 1 medium onion, diced
                    • 1 carrot, sliced
                    • 1 celery stalk, sliced
                    • 2 medium potatoes, diced
                    • 2 garlic cloves, minced
                    • 2 bay leaves
                    • 3 to 4 whole allspice berries
                    • 1/2 teaspoon black pepper
                    • 1/2 teaspoon caraway seeds, optional
                    • Salt, to taste
                    • 1 tablespoon butter or pork fat
                    • Fresh dill or parsley, optional

                    Build the broth 1. Put the pork in a large pot and cover with the water or stock. Bring to a gentle boil, skimming any foam from the surface. 2. Reduce to a low simmer, partially cover, and cook for 45 to 60 minutes, until the pork is tender. 3. Remove the pork, let it cool slightly, then cut it into bite-size pieces and return it to the pot. ## Add sauerkraut and spices 1. Add the sauerkraut, bay leaves, allspice, black pepper, and caraway, if using. 2. Simmer for 30 minutes, allowing the sauerkraut to soften and season the broth. If the sauerkraut is especially sharp, rinse half of it before adding it to the soup. ## Cook the vegetables 1. Melt the butter or pork fat in a small pan. Saute the onion, carrot, and celery over medium heat for 8 to 10 minutes, until soft and lightly golden. 2. Add the garlic and cook for 30 seconds, then transfer the vegetables to the soup. 3. Add the potatoes and kielbasa. Simmer for 20 to 25 minutes, until the potatoes are tender. ## Finish and serve 1. Taste before adding salt, since the sauerkraut and kielbasa are already salty. Add reserved sauerkraut liquid a tablespoon at a time if you want more tang. 2. Remove the bay leaves and allspice. Garnish with dill or parsley, if using. 3. Serve with rye bread or dark sourdough, black pepper at the table, and sour cream on the side if you like. ## Notes The finished soup should be savory and smoky first, with a rounded sauerkraut tang rather than harsh acidity. The ribs make the broth rich and cohesive, while the kielbasa provides distinct smoky bites. Like many soups of this kind, it tastes even better after a day in the refrigerator and freezes well.

                          Roasted Pepper, Chickpea & Orzo Soup

                          A bright, lemony chickpea and orzo soup built around roasted bell peppers, onions, carrots, and plenty of fresh basil.

                          Developed with ChatGPT.

                          Prep: 25 minutes · Cook: 1 hour 30 minutes · Serves: 4 servings

                          Ingredients

                          • 2 bell peppers, seeded and cut into wide strips
                          • 1 yellow onion, cut into wedges
                          • 2 carrots, cut into 2-inch pieces
                          • 3 tablespoons olive oil, divided
                          • Kosher salt and freshly ground black pepper
                          • 2 celery ribs, diced
                          • 3 to 4 garlic cloves, finely chopped
                          • 1 teaspoon ground coriander
                          • 1/4 teaspoon ground turmeric
                          • Pinch of red pepper flakes
                          • 1 cup dried chickpeas, unsoaked
                          • 3 cups water, for cooking the chickpeas
                          • 6 cups vegetable or chicken broth
                          • 3/4 cup orzo
                          • Zest of 1 lemon
                          • 2 to 3 tablespoons fresh lemon juice
                          • 1/2 cup packed fresh basil leaves, torn or thinly sliced
                          • 4 slices bacon, cooked crisp and crumbled (optional)

                          Developed with ChatGPT from a shared chickpea-and-orzo soup idea, then adapted with roasted vegetables and basil as the primary herb. ## Cook the chickpeas 1. Add the dried, unsoaked chickpeas and 3 cups water to an Instant Pot. Seal the lid and cook at high pressure for 35 minutes. 2. Let the pressure release naturally for 15 minutes, then carefully release any remaining pressure. Drain the chickpeas and set aside. They should be tender but still hold their shape. ## Roast the vegetables 1. Heat the oven to 425F. Toss the bell peppers, onion wedges, and carrots with 2 tablespoons olive oil, salt, and pepper on a sheet pan. 2. Roast until the vegetables are tender and browned at the edges, 25 to 30 minutes, turning once halfway through. Set aside; the roasted vegetables are added to the soup later. ## Make the soup 1. If using bacon, cook it in a soup pot or Dutch oven until crisp. Transfer the bacon to a plate, leaving about 1 tablespoon of fat in the pot. If not using bacon, heat the remaining 1 tablespoon olive oil in the pot instead. 2. Add the celery with a pinch of salt and cook until softened, about 4 minutes. Add the garlic, coriander, turmeric, and red pepper flakes; cook until fragrant, about 1 minute. 3. Add the broth, cooked chickpeas, and roasted bell peppers, onions, and carrots. Bring to a simmer and cook for 10 minutes. 4. For a lightly creamy texture, blend 1 to 2 cups of the soup and return it to the pot, or mash some of the chickpeas against the side of the pot. 5. Add the orzo and simmer until tender, 8 to 10 minutes, stirring occasionally. 6. Stir in the lemon zest, lemon juice, and most of the basil. Taste and adjust with salt, pepper, and more lemon juice as needed. ## To serve Ladle into bowls and finish with the remaining basil. Sprinkle with the optional crisp bacon for a smoky, salty contrast. ## Notes - Basil is the lead herb here; use it generously and add it at the end so it stays fresh and aromatic. - The bacon is entirely optional. The soup is complete without it, but the salty crunch is a nice addition. - The recipe begins with dried, unsoaked chickpeas; do not use canned chickpeas for this version.
